# YouTube Titles — Framework
Source: Jake Thomas (Creator Hooks).
Adapted for use inside the content OS.

---

## The Job of a Title

The title confirms for the viewer:
"Yes, this is the video I want."
It does not need to sell the video.
It needs to remove doubt that this is the right video.

---

## Traffic Source Changes Everything

### Home or Recommended (passive browsing)
Viewer is not looking for anything specific.
Title must be simple, clear, and easy to process.
Do not make them work to understand it.
Thumbnail does more of the heavy lifting here.

### Search (active hunting)
Viewer is looking for a specific outcome.
Title must make it clear this video has the answer.
Can afford slightly more detail and keywords.
Title does more of the heavy lifting here.

Default assumption for most channels:
Most early views come from Home or Recommended.
Optimize for clarity and emotional pull first.
Optimize for search keywords second.

---

## The Three Click Emotions

From analysis of approximately 1000 high-performing titles:
- About 60% had curiosity
- About 46% had desire
- About 39% had fear

Rules:
- Curiosity: always present. Every title needs it.
- Desire: default for educational content.
- Fear: use sparingly. Only when it fits content and brand.

Default mix: curiosity plus desire.
Add fear only when the content genuinely warrants it
and the brand's hard constraints allow it.

---

## Length and Clarity

Sweet spot: 45-55 characters, 5-10 words.
Search can handle slightly longer.
Home or Recommended: shorter is almost always better.

The test: can someone understand what this video
is about in under 2 seconds of reading?
If no — simplify.

---

## Title Generation Process

Step 1: Determine traffic intent.
Is this built for search or Home or Recommended?

Step 2: Choose the emotional mix.
Always curiosity. Add desire or fear based on content.

Step 3: Apply length guidance.
Target 45-55 characters.

Step 4: Run the competitor check.
Could a competitor put this title on their video?
If yes — add something only this brand can say.

Step 5: Test timeliness.
Is there a moment that makes this hit harder right now?

## Title Patterns That Work

The Specific Result:
"How I Built [SPECIFIC OUTCOME] With [SPECIFIC CONSTRAINT]"
"[STARTING POINT] to [END POINT] in [TIMEFRAME] — The Exact Mechanic"

The Provocation:
"Why Your [THING] Will Never [OUTCOME]"
"Most [TARGET ROLE] Are Building the Wrong Thing"

The Pattern Interrupt:
"A [UNEXPECTED CREDENTIAL]'s Guide to [TOPIC]"
"I Lost [SPECIFIC STAKE]. Here's What Actually [WORKS]."

The Direct Promise:
"The [TOPIC] System, Step by Step"
"How to Turn [STATE A] Into [STATE B]"

The Curiosity Gap:
"What [UNEXPECTED REFERENCE] Never Had to Build (And Why You Do)"
"Why the Best [THINGS] Have Nothing to Do With [OBVIOUS ANSWER]"

---

## Title Anti-Patterns (never use)

- Vague: "My thoughts on [topic]"
- Clickbait with no delivery: "This changed everything"
- ALL CAPS screaming: "THIS WILL BLOW YOUR MIND"
- Keyword stuffing: multiple keywords crammed together
- Hollow promise: "The secret to going viral" without delivery

---

## Timeliness

Use timeliness when:
- There is a clear intersection with the brand's core themes
- The moment is at or just before peak, not after

Do not use for:
- Evergreen pillar content
- Origin story or building-in-public content
- Anything that should rank for years, not days
