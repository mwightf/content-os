# Before-After-Bridge (BAB)

## Source
Classic copywriting framework.
Works by showing the transformation before explaining
how to get there.

---

## The Three Beats

BEFORE:
Paint the world the reader lives in right now.
Specific and recognizable. They should think "that's me."

AFTER:
Paint the world they want to live in.
Do not explain how to get there yet.
Just make the destination vivid and real.

BRIDGE:
Show how to get from before to after.
This is where the product, system, or content lives.

---

## When to Use BAB

Best fit:
- Channel descriptions and bios
- Video intros (first 30-45 seconds)
- Newsletter welcome sequences
- Landing pages

---

## Template

BEFORE:
[2-4 lines in the reader's voice describing
their current state. Use their words.
Specific frustration, not generic pain.]

AFTER:
[2-4 lines painting the destination.
Concrete and vivid. What changes in their
day, week, or business when they get there.
No how-to yet — just the picture.]

BRIDGE:
[2-4 lines naming the system, framework, or
content that bridges the gap.
Pull from the channel tagline and core promise
in 01_brand/brand_index.md.]

---

## BAB vs PAS

PAS: leads with pain. Agitates before solving.
Best when the audience needs to feel the urgency first.

BAB: leads with contrast. Shows the destination early.
Best when the audience needs to see the possibility first.

For an ICP that already feels the pain (most do):
BAB often works better because they need to see
the destination is real, not just feel the problem again.
