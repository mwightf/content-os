# Ideal Customer Profile — Matt Khorsandi (GLP Marketing)

## Primary ICP: The Plateaued Managing Partner

**Who they are:**
Managing partner of a US law firm doing $5M-$50M in annual
revenue. Between 40 and 60. Built the firm themselves or
inherited it from a parent. 15-100 attorneys plus support
staff. Profitable but plateauing. Already been burned by
at least one marketing agency.

**Situation:**
Revenue $5M-$50M. Established, profitable, growth-plateaued.
Marketing spend usually $20K-$150K/month split across an
underperforming agency, Google Ads, and a junior in-house
marketer. Outdated website. Neglected Google Business
Profile. Inconsistent or zero content output. No AI
visibility strategy. No real CMO function.

**What they want:**
To be the obvious dominant firm in their market. To stop
worrying about case flow. To know what's working without
becoming a marketing expert themselves. To finally fire
the agency that's been gaslighting them for two years.
To stop being intimidated by terms like "schema markup"
and "AI Overviews." To be respected by their peers as the
firm that figured growth out.

**What they've tried:**
Google Ads burning $30K-$80K/month with unclear ROI. A
"full-service" legal marketing agency delivering monthly
reports full of impressions and bounce rates. A junior
in-house marketer learning on their dime. An SEO firm
that promised page-one rankings six months ago with nothing
to show. LinkedIn ads. Facebook ads. Maybe a TV spot they
regret. A friend's nephew who "knows social media."

**What they lie awake thinking about:**
"Will next quarter's case volume hold? Is my best associate
about to leave and start a competing firm? Is the marketing
money I'm spending actually doing anything? Will the firm
I built still be relevant in 5 years when AI changes how
clients find lawyers? Sell, scale, or stay still?"

**What they secretly want to believe:**
That they can dominate their market without becoming a
marketing expert themselves. That there's a straight-talking
operator out there who will tell them the truth and tie
the work to outcomes.

**Platform:**
LinkedIn first. X for the more operator-minded. Email always.
YouTube on weekends. Reachable through an assistant and a
well-researched personal Loom.

---

## Secondary ICP: Rising Partners and Marketing Leads Inside Firms

**Who they are:**
Younger partners and rising marketing leaders inside
law firms. Don't have buying authority yet but they bring
Matt to the managing partner. Consume more content, share
more, become future ICPs as they take on more decision power.

**What they want:**
Playbooks they can bring upstairs. The vocabulary to
explain SEO and AI visibility to a senior partner without
sounding like an intern. Career capital from being the
person who saw it first.

**Platform:** LinkedIn, X, YouTube

---

## Tertiary ICP: Operators and Fractional CMOs

**Who they are:**
Other agency operators and fractional CMO types. Not buyers
but they amplify the message, refer clients, and validate
the operator brand. The audience for the "building GLP in
public" content.

**What they want:**
The operator playbook. How GLP runs, hires, prices, retains.
What's working in 2026 and 2027.

**Platform:** X, YouTube, LinkedIn

---

## Cross-ICP Truth

All three ICPs share one belief:
The marketing industry is broken and most of the people
pitching them are full of it. They want a straight-talking
operator who will tell them the truth and tie work to outcomes.

---

## Awareness Level

- Problem aware — they know agencies are gaslighting them
- Solution unaware — they don't know what an operator-grade
  marketing function actually looks like
- Not product aware — most haven't heard of GLP yet

Content primarily speaks to problem-aware partners and
moves them toward solution awareness (owned channels,
fractional CMO function, AI visibility as the next decade's
moat).

---

## What the ICP Is NOT

- Solo attorneys under $1M who want a magic bullet
- Firms that won't commit past 90 days
- Personal injury volume mills wanting spammy lead gen
- Anyone shopping on price
- Anyone who thinks paid ads are the growth lever
- Partners who delegate marketing to a junior and disappear
- Anyone who treats us like a vendor instead of a partner
- Anyone who can't take direct feedback
