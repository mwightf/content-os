## The Master Test: 3 Questions for Every Line

1. Can I visualize it?
   If you can't see it, readers won't either.

2. Can I falsify it?
   If it can't be true or false, it's fluff.

3. Can nobody else say this?
   If a competitor could sign it, rewrite it.

Three yes = keep it. Any no = rewrite it.

---

## Rule 1: Make It Visual

**The drop test:**
- Abstract = can't drop it on your foot
- Concrete = can drop it on your foot

**The zoom technique:**
Abstract to concrete — keep zooming until it becomes a picture.

"Stronger community" becomes
"40-200 people who show up every Friday and feel weird if they miss it."

"Better distribution" becomes
"0 to 300k views in 4 weeks on a new account."

When you write a vague phrase, ask:
- What would this look like on camera?
- What is the specific number behind this?
- What scene proves this is true?

---

## Rule 2: Make It Falsifiable

Don't talk. Point.

Instead of: "Our community is high-value."
Point to: "Members have collectively closed millions
in deals from relationships formed inside."

For every claim, ask:
- Could someone check if this is true?
- What is the screenshot, graph, or story that proves it?
- Can I replace this adjective with a measurable fact?

**Rewrites:**
"Fast growth" becomes a specific before/after number.
"Trusted leader" becomes a specific story or credential.

---

## Rule 3: Make It Yours Alone

If a competitor could swap their name in — rewrite it.

Three places to find what only you can say:
1. Your origin story
2. Your actual results (specific numbers from the fact bank)
3. Your best customers' reality

The check: print the line and cover the name.
If it could be signed by anyone else in your space —
the line is generic. Add a number, a story, or a
specific you that nobody else can match.

---

## Rule 4: One Idea Per Piece

Every piece contains one idea. Not two. One.

The one-sentence test:
Can you state the core idea in one sentence?
If not — keep cutting.

---

## Rule 5: Structure Is Invisible Infrastructure

**Short form (post):**
Hook, Insight, Proof, Close.

**Medium form (thread, newsletter section):**
Hook, Context, Framework, Example, Close.

**Long form (article, video script):**
Hook, Problem, Agitation, Solution,
Proof, Framework, Application, Close.

**Conflict as backbone:**
The most powerful structure is always A vs B.

"Most [people] do [X].
The ones that win do [Y]."

---

## Rule 6: Simple Writing Is Rewriting

Kaplan's Law:
Any word that isn't working for you is working against you.

Paragraph rules:
- Max 2-3 lines in short-form
- Every sentence depends on the one before it
- Remove a sentence — if nothing breaks, cut it

---

## Rule 7: Facts as Anchors

Start with a fact. Then ask: so what?

Facts come from the brand fact bank in
brand_index.md — never invented at draft time.
If a number isn't in the fact bank, it doesn't get used.

---

## Rule 8: The Close Is a Decision Point

Every piece ends with the reader in one of three states:
1. They know what to do next
2. They see something differently
3. They feel something they want to share

**Close types:**
Action: "Pick one platform. Go deep. Report back."
Reframe: "You don't have a content problem.
You have a depth problem."
Open loop: "Next week: the exact system behind [result]."
Reflection: "If your brand disappeared tomorrow —
would anyone fight for it?"

---

## Rule 9: AI Plus Experience Equals Edge

Use AI for: first drafts, variations, structure testing.
Never let AI replace: your specific stories, real numbers,
earned point of view.

---

## Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Passes 3-question master test
- [ ] One clear idea
- [ ] Hook earns the next line
- [ ] At least one concrete proof point
- [ ] Practical takeaway present
- [ ] Close tells reader what to think, feel, or do
- [ ] Could not be signed by a competitor
- [ ] Sounds like the brand voice, not a template
